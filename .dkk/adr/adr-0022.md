# ADR-0022 — Shot checks by a vision model

## Context

Plan 06 item 8. Every consistency measure so far (references per model in adr-0018, keyframes in adr-0019, chained
shots in adr-0020, pinned voices in adr-0021) raises the odds of a good take but does not tell the creator which takes
went wrong. In a long episode the misses are easy to overlook until the merge: an extra person or a twin, a character in
other clothes, the wrong kind of place, a melted hand or a subtitle burnt into the frame. A chat model that reads images
can spot these from a few frames beside the references, for a fraction of what a video costs. The plan sent the frames
to the text service's model and asked for an identity score per character; neither holds up. The text service's
default model need not read images, and a general chat model is not a face verifier: a score from 0 to 5 would look
precise while being noise.

## Decision

- **Service.** `vision` is a service type: a chat model that reads images, on OpenAI, Gemini, Ark, BytePlus or
  ModelRunner (where the catalog lists text-to-text models that take image input). It is optional like speech and lip
  sync: readiness never waits for it, the settings page marks it Optional with its own hint, and nothing but the check
  uses it. Quick setup on ModelRunner adds `deepseek/v4.1-flash` (it takes images); the OpenAI and Gemini presets offer
  `gpt-5` and `gemini-2.5-flash`. A request may name `visionServiceId` and `model`; otherwise the highest-priority
  active vision service and its first model are used. There is no episode lock, and no temperature is sent.
- **Inputs.** Two JPEG contact sheets made with ffmpeg: three frames of the current video, at a sixth, half and five
  sixths of its length (clear of the fade-in and of the still ending a continued shot is asked for), each scaled into a
  512 px box; and the references, each bound character's full body, else its cover (the first four characters), then
  the scene's image, each padded to a 512 px square. The message gives the expected cast (every bound character;
  narrators are not bound), a legend for the reference strip, the shot's description and the language for the notes
  (the content language).
- **Rubric.** Four points, each `ok` with a one-sentence note, as JSON only: **people** (more people than the cast, or
  the same person twice; fewer is fine), **outfit** (clothing clearly unlike its reference, not light or angle),
  **scene** (clearly another kind of place or time of day), **artifacts** (deformed faces or hands, extra limbs, melting
  objects, text, logos, watermarks, broken frames). The model is told not to judge whether faces match. Code sets a
  point `skipped` when there is nothing to compare with: outfit without a pictured character, scene without a scene
  image. The verdict is `check` when any point is flagged, else `ok`.
- **CheckShot (`POST /shots/:id/qa`).** Synchronous: the request waits for the answer (at most 90 s) and returns the
  shot. It is refused (412) without a vision service, without a video, or when the video file is missing or unreadable;
  a shot of a deleted episode is not found (404).
  A rejected key, a timeout or an answer that is not the check is a provider error, and nothing is stored.
- **Storage (migration 0013).** `shots.qa` holds the video path judged, the time, the model, the verdict and the four
  points. It is written only while the shot still plays the video that was judged, and replaces the earlier check. A
  check is shown only while its video is the shot's current one: a new video, a lip sync or another version picked from
  history hides it, and picking the judged version again shows it again unless another version was checked meanwhile.
- **A signal, never a gate.** Nothing reads the check but the studio: generation, batches and the merge ignore it.
- **Studio.** The shot list shows "Needs a look" on a flagged shot and an icon on a fine one. The inspector shows the
  verdict, each point with its note (or why it was skipped), the model, and Check / Check again. The stage header checks
  every done video without a check ("Check N videos"), or the selected shots in select mode, two at a time, with a
  summary at the end.
- **Offline stub.** The stub model answers the check for any request with images and no tools, and flags artifacts on
  roughly a third of the shots, the same ones each time, so the flagged path can be seen without a provider.

## Alternatives Considered

- **The text service's model, as planned.** Many text models do not read images, and a check would fail or, worse,
  answer without looking. A separate type lets the creator pick a model that sees, and a cheaper one than the agents use.
- **Identity scores per character.** A chat model cannot verify faces reliably, and a number invites gating on noise.
  Face-embedding metrics (ArcFace-style recognition models, DINO similarity) measure identity properly but need a local
  model runtime, and the common pretrained face models are licensed for non-commercial research only. Backlog.
- **Each frame and reference as its own image.** More image tokens, and some endpoints limit images per message; two
  sheets with a legend carry the same information.
- **Sending the video itself.** Only some providers take video input; frames work on every vision model.
- **A generation task with polling and a history of checks.** A check takes seconds and costs little, and it belongs to
  one video; a synchronous call and one stored check are enough.
- **Checking every video automatically, and regenerating flagged shots up to three times.** Spends money unasked and
  multiplies video costs on a noisy signal; the creator decides what to redo.
- **A review queue.** The badge, the batch and the inspector cover it for now.

## Consequences

- A creator can sweep an episode for obvious misses before the merge, at the cost of one short chat call per shot.
- Faces are not checked: identity drift still needs a human eye, and the check is only as good as the vision model.
  False flags and misses happen, which is why the check never blocks anything.
- A check holds the request open for up to 90 s; the batch sends two at a time.
- A check follows the video only: changing the shot's cast, description or a reference image leaves the earlier check
  in place, and Check again judges against the new ones.
- The outfit point compares with the character's full body. Episode outfits (deferred from item 1) would be flagged
  until the check uses them.
- No MCP tool requests checks yet, the Ark and BytePlus presets suggest no vision model, and the vision defaults have
  not been tried against live providers (backlog).

## Amendment (2026-10-17)

**Episode outfits are checked against the outfit** (adr-0023). In an episode where a character wears another outfit,
its full-body reference is the outfit picture (labelled as this episode's outfit) and the message carries the wardrobe
line, so a deliberate change of clothes is no longer flagged. With an outfit but no picture the reference is the
character's cover (its face), and the outfit is judged from the wardrobe line alone.
