# ADR-0020 — Continuous takes between shots

## Context

Plan 06 item 7. A storyboard often splits one moment across two or three shots: a walk that goes on, a reaction held
from the same angle. Generated separately, each shot is a new take: people stand elsewhere, light shifts and the cut
shows. Models that take a first frame (adr-0019) can start a video on a given still, so a shot can start on the last
frame of the shot before and the two play as one take. Two things push back. A continued shot sees no reference image,
only the frame, so identity drifts a little at every link. And its first frame does not exist until the shot before it
is generated, which may still be running when the creator, or a batch, asks for it.

## Decision

- A shot carries `continues_previous` (migration 0011), set by the creator in the studio, by `update_shot`, or by the
  storyboard breaker. The shot it continues is the previous live shot by number; the first shot's mark means nothing.
- At most two shots in a row continue the one before. The third is refused, so faces are reset from references at
  least every third shot.
- RequestShotVideo for a continuing shot needs a model that takes a first frame and no reference video or audio, and a
  previous shot with a video or one generating; otherwise it is refused (412, 400) rather than quietly made as a cut.
  It sends no reference images, opens the prompt with an unbroken-take sentence, keeps the headcount and wardrobe of
  the people the plan would have sent, and asks them to keep their look from the first frame. The shot's keyframe is
  not used. A shot the next one continues is asked to end on a steady moment, so the frame taken from it is clean.
- The task is claimed at once (so the shot reads as generating and the response can say it is waiting) and, before it
  takes an episode slot, waits in process, polling every 3 s, while the previous shot's video task is processing. It
  stops when its own task is cancelled, and fails when the previous task fails. It also fails, before anything is
  sent, when the shot no longer continues that shot (the mark was removed or the shots were reordered while it
  waited). It then takes the frame a quarter
  second (6 frames at 24 fps) before the end of what the film shows of that clip, the shorter of its length and the
  length it was asked for, stores it as an image and records it on the task with `chainedFrom` (shot, task, video and
  time).
- The studio's batch sends a continuing shot's request after the request for the shot before it has been answered, in
  story order, so the API finds that video generating.
- The merge joins a clip to the clip before it when it continued that very video: the one before ends on the frame
  taken, the continuing clip starts one frame in (past the shared frame) and takes the grade of the one before, since
  its colours came from that clip ungraded. A clip that continued a version not in the film is matched to its shot's
  keyframe only. The encoder line records the joins.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Fall back to references when a shot cannot continue.** The keyframe does this (adr-0019), but a keyframe is a
  refinement while a continuous take is a story decision: a silent cut where one was asked for is a worse surprise than
  a refusal that names the reason.
- **A durable dependency between tasks, redispatched at boot.** A waiting task holds nothing at the provider, so like a
  task waiting for a slot it fails at boot and is requested again. A dependency column and a scheduler would add a
  second dispatch path for a rare restart.
- **Wait in the client before requesting.** The batch would need to poll each shot's video before sending the next,
  and a single request from the inspector would have to be refused while the shot before it runs. Waiting on the
  server serves both and keeps the task visible.
- **The very last frame.** Generated clips often fade, freeze or blur in their last frames; a quarter second earlier is
  steadier, and the merge cuts the clip before it at that frame so nothing repeats.
- **Reference images alongside the frame.** First-frame endpoints take the frame alone (adr-0019); identity is held by
  the cap of two continued shots instead.
- **Longer chains.** Each link compounds drift; two continued shots (a three-shot take, up to 45 s) cover the moments
  that need it.

## Consequences

- A continued shot keeps position, light and motion across the cut, at the cost of a small identity drift and of being
  generated after the shot before it.
- Regenerating the shot before leaves an already generated continuing shot joined to the old version: the merge then
  cuts between them and matches it to its keyframe. Regenerating the continuing shot picks up the new frame.
- A server restart fails continuing shots that were still waiting; they are requested again.
- On a model without a first frame, continuing shots cannot be generated until the mark is removed or the model
  changed; the studio says so before anything is requested.
