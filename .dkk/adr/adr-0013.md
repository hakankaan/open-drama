# ADR-0013 — Model providers: official endpoints of supported models, plus BytePlus and ModelRunner

## Context

Image and video generation go through provider adapters (`adr-0005`). The product owner has decided which providers the app accepts in its model-service configuration; some of them are delivered in a later iteration.

## Decision

The configurable provider set is:

1. **The official endpoints of every model family the app supports** — OpenAI (text, images), Gemini (text, images), Volcengine Ark / Seedance (text, images, video), MiniMax (video), Alibaba Bailian / Wan (video).
2. **BytePlus** — ByteDance's international platform (ModelArk), the international counterpart of Volcengine Ark for the Seedance and Seedream model families. Expected to reuse the Volcengine adapters with BytePlus base URLs and any endpoint differences handled in the adapter.
3. **ModelRunner** — the product owner's own inference gateway, as a single adapter covering images and video (submit a run, poll the request, download the result), and text through its OpenAI-compatible surface where available.

**Delivery:** the official providers ship in iteration 1 (Plan 2, Phases E–F). The **BytePlus and ModelRunner adapters are deferred to iteration 2** and tracked in `docs/BACKLOG.md` so they are not forgotten. The adapter registry, the provider enum and the connectivity probes are designed so that adding them is one adapter file plus a probe each.

## Alternatives Considered

- **ModelRunner as the only provider.** Not chosen: creators bring their own keys for official endpoints; ModelRunner is an addition, not a replacement.
- **Shipping BytePlus in iteration 1 as a Volcengine alias.** Deferred by the product owner to keep iteration 1 focused; the alias approach is the likely implementation when it lands.

## Consequences

- `ProviderName` in the contract enum gains `byteplus` and `modelrunner` now (so configuration and UI templates can mention them), with the adapters marked "coming in iteration 2" in the settings UI until they exist.
- Quick-setup templates for BytePlus and ModelRunner are written together with their adapters.
- The video-provider question that sat on milestone M3's critical path is resolved: iteration 1 ships Seedance (Volcengine), MiniMax and Wan in that order of priority unless the owner reorders them.

## Amendment (2026-09-29)

The product owner brought **BytePlus into iteration 1**: the first keyed check ran on a BytePlus ModelArk key. It ships as the Volcengine alias described above: the registry serves `byteplus` with the Ark image and video adapters, text uses the OpenAI-compatible client, the probe shares the Ark request, and quick setup offers a BytePlus template next to the Volcengine one. The same day ModelRunner followed (below).

## Amendment 2 (2026-09-29)

The product owner brought **ModelRunner into iteration 1** as well, so no provider adapter is deferred any more. Text goes through ModelRunner's OpenAI-compatible chat route (`/v1`) with the OpenAI-compatible client; thinking is turned off there with `reasoning_effort: "none"`. Images and video use one adapter on the queue API (`Authorization: Key`, submit, poll the request's result path on the service's own origin, download). ModelRunner splits a model family by mode, so the service names one endpoint and the adapter uses its sibling when references are present (`…/text-to-image` ↔ `…/edit`, `…/text-to-video` ↔ `…/reference-to-video`); it drives the Seedream and Seedance families. The probe checks the key without submitting work.

## Amendment 3 (2026-09-29)

**ModelRunner is the recommended quick-setup gateway**: its template is listed first and marked as recommended, because one key covers text, image and video across many model families. The other templates stay selectable.

**Video limits are per model.** `videoCapsFor(provider, model)` starts from the provider's defaults and applies the first matching row of `VIDEO_MODEL_CAPS`. Sources: the Seedance 2.5 tutorials of Volcengine Ark and BytePlus ModelArk (model comparison table: Seedance 2.0 renders up to 1080p, 2.0 Fast and Mini up to 720p, 2.5 up to 1080p with 4–30 s, 30 images, 10 videos, 10 audio, and audio-only references), ModelRunner's per-endpoint input schemas (Seedance 2.5 there: 480p/720p, 4–30 s, 30 images, 10 audio; the Fast and Mini tiers have no reference endpoint), and the MiniMax V2 video reference (H3-Max: 5–15 s, 480P and 768P). The shot request fits the episode's resolution to the model's tiers (the highest one up to the request, else the lowest), so adapters receive a resolution their model renders.

## Amendment 4 (2026-09-29)

**The ModelRunner service dialog browses the live catalog.** `GET /model-services/modelrunner-catalog?type=` reads ModelRunner's public, keyless catalog on every request and returns the endpoints a service of that type can use with their USD prices. Nothing is stored, because the catalog's terms forbid compiling a copy. Text lists every `text-to-text` model. Image and video list only what the adapter maps, decided by the adapter's own rule (`offersEndpoint`). Picking one adds it to the service's models. Limits still come from `VIDEO_MODEL_CAPS`; the catalog's input schemas are not read into them.

**The ModelRunner adapter names its versions.** It drives Seedream 5 and 5 Pro and Seedance 2.0, 2.5, 2.0 Fast and 2.0 Mini. Older versions take other inputs (Seedream 4.5 `image_size`, Seedance 1.5 a string `duration`), and Seedance 1.5 has no reference endpoint to switch to. The adapter refuses them as a configuration error rather than letting the provider reject the request.

**Seedance 2.5 on Ark confirmed.** A keyed BytePlus Seedance 2.5 clip (4 s, one reference image) was accepted with `omni_reference_task_type: "reference"` and followed its reference.

## Amendment 5 (2026-10-05)

**The ModelRunner adapter reads each endpoint's input schema instead of naming versions.** This replaces amendment 4's version list and its "limits still come from `VIDEO_MODEL_CAPS`".

- **What is read, and when.** The adapter reads the public, keyless `GET /models/{endpoint}/openapi.json` (`components.schemas.Input`) for the endpoints a service uses, at the moment one is used. The answer is kept in memory for an hour, at most 200 endpoints, and is never written anywhere.
- **Why this fits the terms.** It takes nothing from the catalog beyond what the request being built needs, so it is not a compiled copy (§6(e)(xiv)). The catalog picker still reads only the catalog list and never a per-entry schema.
- **When a read fails.** If a refresh fails, the expired answer keeps serving and the read is retried a minute later.

**Mapping inputs by role.** The adapter maps each input to a role, using both its name and its schema type:
- the prompt
- the length in seconds: an integer range, integer literals, or string literals (sent as a string)
- the resolution: literals mapped to a height, and a tier is offered only if it maps back to itself
- the shape: `aspect_ratio` or `ratio`
- the audio flag: `generate_audio` or `audio`
- reference images: a list field, or numbered `reference_image_N` slots; the maximum comes from `maxItems` or the field's description
- reference audio
- the image size: `size` or `image_size`, chosen by ratio and then the pixel count closest to 2K
- the mention style: `@Image1`, `[Image 1]` or the plain name, as the prompt's description shows it

The text-only and reference siblings are found by name, as before. Bare `owner/alias` image endpoints pair with `…/edit`.

**The adapter refuses rather than guesses.** It raises a configuration error, which fails the task as `config` and returns `412` on the request path, when the endpoint:
- does not exist
- has no prompt
- has a required input the adapter does not fill (for example an endpoint that needs a start frame)
- takes no length in seconds
- takes no shape, when the episode's shape is not the shape that endpoint renders by default

**Exact shapes for video.** An endpoint that takes a shape receives the exact literal or the request is refused. Images take the nearest ratio. An endpoint without a shape input (Veo's reference mode) is assumed to render its text sibling's default shape, and any other shape is refused before anything is paid.

**Limits are learned.** `describeModel` returns the pair's limits; where both endpoints state a limit, they are combined to what both accept. The fields are:
- the reference counts
- `audioNeedsVisual`
- the length range, plus the discrete lengths when the allowed values have gaps
- the resolution tiers
- the shapes

`videoCapsFor` stays the fallback, and the merge order is: provider defaults ← `VIDEO_MODEL_CAPS` ← learned limits. ModelRunner no longer has hand-kept rows.

`GET /video-models/caps?provider&model` serves the merged limits to the studio, which offers only those tiers and clamps lengths to them. A shot request reads the limits before it checks the shape and length. The storyboard agent asks for lengths the model renders. If the limits cannot be read, the agents and the route fall back to the table with a warning.

**What the catalog picker lists.** It lists:
- video endpoints in the `text-to-video` category, plus `…/reference-to-video` endpoints
- image endpoints in the `text-to-image` category (vectors left out), plus `…/edit` endpoints

Checked on 2026-10-05: the generated request bodies for 15 image endpoints and every listed video family validated against their schemas. LongCat is listed but refused, because it needs a start frame. Keyed runs the same day: Veo 3.1 reference-to-video (two data-URL references, 720p 16:9, rendered 8 s) and Happy Horse 1.1 reference-to-video (two numbered slots with `[Image N]` mentions, 3 s, 720p; its four-segment endpoint id polled through `alibaba/happy-horse`) both finished and followed their references.
