# ADR-0021 — Pinned voices: speech, line audio and lip sync

## Context

Plan 06 item 5. A video model that makes its own sound gives a character a new voice in every shot: timbre, pace and
accent drift from one take to the next, and dialogue may come out in the wrong language or not at all. Faces are held
by references (adr-0018) and first frames (adr-0019, adr-0020); voices have no such anchor, since few video models take
reference audio and none keeps a voice across separate requests. A speech model reads a line in one fixed voice (named,
or cloned from a recording) every time, and a lip-sync model re-draws a speaker's mouth over a finished video to match
given audio. Together they let a voice be decided once per character and kept for the whole series.

## Decision

- **Data (migration 0012).** A character has a `voice` (one of the speech model's named voices, `''` for none) and a
  `voiceSamplePath` (an uploaded recording, in the `audio` bucket). A shot has one spoken `line` (at most 1000
  characters) and a `speakerId`, which must be one of its bound characters (never a narrator, who speaks over the
  picture rather than in it); unbinding the speaker clears it. A shot has `lineAudioPath` and `lineAudioSeconds`. A drama
  has `pinnedVoices` (default false). Changing a line or speaker clears that shot's line audio; changing a character's
  voice or sample clears the line audio of every shot it speaks in. The storyboard breaker fills `speakerId` and `line`
  through `save_shots`, one speaker per shot.
- **Services.** `speech` and `lipsync` are service types, driven through ModelRunner (and the offline stub). They are
  optional: readiness still needs only text, image and video, the settings page marks them Optional, and the quick
  setup offers `elevenlabs/tts/multilingual-v2` and `bytedance/latentsync`. Each request uses the highest-priority active
  service; there is no episode lock. The speech adapter reads the endpoint's schema for its text, voice, clone and
  language fields; its named voices come from the schema (`GET /speech/voices`), and a model with a clone field clones.
- **Line audio (RequestLineAudio, `POST /shots/:id/line-audio`).** A speech task (shot-owned, polled every 3 s within
  10 minutes) reads the line in the content language: cloned from the speaker's sample where the model clones, else in
  the speaker's named voice. When nothing is cloned, a speech model that takes no named voice is refused (412) rather
  than reading the line in a voice of its own. The result attaches (AttachLineAudioOnGeneration) only while the line,
  the speaker, the voice and the sample are what was asked for; otherwise it stays on the task. Attaching grows the
  shot to hold a 0.5 s lead-in, the line and a 0.5 s tail (`ceil(0.5 + seconds + 0.5)`, at most 30 s), never shrinks
  it, marks the video prompt stale when it grew, and recomputes the episode's length.
- **Pinned video (RequestShotVideo).** With pinned voices, a shot with a line is refused (412) without a speaker or
  without its line audio, when the lead-in and line do not fit the model's longest video, and when no lip-sync service
  is configured, all before anything is paid. Its length grows to hold the lead-in and line, it is made without sound,
  and the prompt keeps the speaker's face in view. A shot without a line is told nobody speaks. The task records the
  line audio it was sized for (`autoLipSync`).
- **Automatic lip sync (LipSyncPinnedVideo).** When that video is stored, a lip sync is requested for it. If the line
  audio changed meanwhile, or the sync cannot start, a failed lip-sync task (`errorClass` `config`) records why, so the
  shot shows why its video has no voice. Nothing is requested once the shot's episode (or its drama) was deleted.
- **Lip sync (RequestLipSync, `POST /shots/:id/lip-sync`).** A lipsync task (shot-owned, polled every 5 s within 30
  minutes) starts from the shot's current video or, when that is itself a lip sync, from the video it was made from, so
  a re-spoken line is never synced over a synced copy. It is refused (412) without line audio and when the lead-in and
  line run longer than that video, and (409) while the shot has a video or lip sync processing: `video` and `lipsync`
  are one task family for history, the merge, chaining and the busy check. The line is padded to the video's length (0.5
  s lead-in, the line, silence) as mono AAC. The provider fetches its inputs from `PUBLIC_BASE_URL`, else from the
  source video's provider URL while it is under 45 minutes old, else as a data URI (at most 25 MB). The result's picture
  is kept with the padded line muxed over it, since a provider may resample or cut its own track. It becomes the shot's
  current video (AttachLipSyncOnGeneration) only while the shot still plays the video the sync was asked for and has the
  same line audio; otherwise it joins the history. The task carries the source task's length, first frame and chain
  link, so the merge treats it as the same take (adr-0020).
- **Without pinned voices**, the speaker's voice sample goes to the video model as reference audio where the model takes
  audio and can fetch it (`PUBLIC_BASE_URL`), never beside a first frame: a style anchor, not a guarantee.
- **Studio.** A voice picker and sample upload on the character; speaker and line in the shot editor; Speak line and Lip
  sync in the inspector, with the audio player, a lip-synced badge on history versions and the reason a sync failed; a
  Pinned voices switch in project settings.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Keep the video model's own audio, steered by reference audio.** Few models take reference audio, and those that do
  imitate it loosely; voices still drift from shot to shot. It stays as the interim anchor when voices are not pinned.
- **Generate the video with sound, then replace the sound.** The mouth would move to other words.
- **Audio-driven video (talking-head and audio-to-video models).** They tie the shot to a narrow set of models and
  framings; a lip sync works on the output of any video model and keeps the references, first frames and chains of
  adr-0018 to adr-0020.
- **Several speakers or a timed dialogue track per shot.** One line per shot keeps the line audio, the length and the
  sync simple; the breaker splits a conversation across shots.
- **Shrinking a shot to its line.** The creator or the breaker chose the length for the action; the line only sets a
  minimum.
- **Syncing a longer line onto an existing video.** The end of the line would be cut off; the video is made again
  instead, and both the automatic and the manual sync refuse.
- **Making a lip sync current whenever it completes.** A sync that finished after the creator picked another version
  or re-spoke the line would silently replace their choice; it joins the history instead.

## Consequences

- A character sounds the same in every pinned shot of the series, in the content language, at the cost of a speech run
  per line and a lip-sync run per spoken video.
- A pinned spoken shot plays only its line: the video is made silent, so its ambience and sound effects are lost until
  ambience is laid under lip-synced shots (backlog). A narrator's voice-over is not covered.
- Re-speaking a line after its video was made needs the video made again when the new line is longer; the inspector
  says so.
- Lip sync needs a face in every frame; the prompt asks for it, and a provider that finds none fails the task.
- The agents' `save_shots` and `update_shot` cannot clear a speaker (they drop null fields); an empty line silences the
  shot all the same, and the studio clears both.
- The MCP tools do not yet request line audio or lip syncs, and the studio has no speech or lip-sync model picker: the
  highest-priority active service is used (backlog).
