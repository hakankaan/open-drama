# ADR-0019 — Shot keyframes and the finishing pass

## Context

`adr-0017` and `adr-0018` steer what a video model is sent, but each shot is still generated from reference images alone, so the first frame a creator sees is the first one they pay for, and nothing ties one shot's faces, outfits and light to the next. The research behind plan 06 found two cheap levers left. A **keyframe** (a still of the shot's opening, drawn by the image model from the same references) can be checked across the episode before any video is paid for, and every model Open Drama drives that takes a first frame then starts from exactly that picture. A **finishing pass** can make the cuts agree after the fact: clips arrive at different sizes, frame rates, colour casts and loudness, and the merge so far only joined them.

What a model takes as a first frame differs by provider. ModelRunner's live schemas (read 2026-10-16) name it per endpoint: Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 `image-to-video` take `image` (2.5 only in `adaptive` shape), Kling 2.6 and Wan 2.7 take `start_image_url`, Veo 3.1 takes `image_url`, and the first/last-frame endpoints add `end_image`, `end_image_url` or `last_frame_url`. Ark's Seedance takes a content item with `role: "first_frame"`, documented by third-party guides rather than the official reference, which also say a first frame may not be mixed with reference images. MiniMax and Bailian are not wired for a first frame.

## Decision

- **A shot has a keyframe** (`shots.keyframe_path`, migration 0010). `RequestShotKeyframe` (`POST /shots/:id/keyframe`) is a shot-owned image task in the episode's locked image service and the drama's aspect ratio. Its references are each bound character's face (headshot, else the image it has; at most four, those the prompts mention first, as the video plan picks them), the scene, the full bodies and the props, in that order; the image engine prefixes a legend naming each image the model actually takes. The prompt is the request's, else the shot's **image prompt, reused as the keyframe prompt**, else one composed from the framing, the first `Shot 1` beat of the video prompt (else the description) and the location; the code adds the style, a headcount, a wardrobe line, the scene's lighting and identity and clean-frame sentences. With characters bound, an image model that takes no reference image fails the task before anything is paid. The result becomes the keyframe; it can be replaced by an upload or removed (`UpdateShot.keyframePath`, a stored file only), and its task cannot be deleted while it is the keyframe.
- **A video starts from the keyframe where the model takes a first frame** (`VideoProviderCaps.firstFrame`: the stub and Ark yes; ModelRunner learned from the model's `image-to-video` sibling; MiniMax and Bailian no). `RequestShotVideo.useKeyframe` defaults to true; reference video or audio turns it off. The keyframe, fitted in 1920 px as a JPEG, goes **alone, without reference images**, since it was drawn from them and providers reject or blur the mix; the prompt keeps the headcount and wardrobe of the people the plan would have sent and asks them to keep their look from the first frame. The shot's own uploaded images are reported as not sent; nothing else is reported as over the limit. The response says `sent`, `off` or `unsupported`, and the version records the keyframe it started from (`ShotVideo.firstFrame`). ModelRunner chooses among three modes (plain, referenced, framed) across the sibling endpoints and sends the drama's shape where the framed endpoint takes it, else `adaptive`/`auto`.
- **The studio shows keyframes side by side.** The shot inspector draws, redraws, removes and opens the keyframe and says whether the target model will start from it; the shot editor holds the keyframe prompt and says when no reference image will be sent. A keyframe sheet shows every shot's keyframe in a grid and draws the missing ones in one batch (four at a time).
- **The finishing pass** (`compositing/render.ts`): every clip is fitted, letterboxed, into the drama's frame at the episode's resolution (16:9 at 720p is 1280×720; an adaptive drama keeps the first clip's frame) at 24 fps. With `MergeShots.colorMatch` (default true) each clip is nudged towards the keyframe it started from, else its shot's current keyframe: per-channel mean and spread of the keyframe and of the clip's first seconds (2 frames a second, at most 24, at 64×64), moved half the way, at most 16 levels and a gain of 0.85 to 1.15, applied with `lutrgb`. A clip with neither keyframe stays as it is; the scene's establishing shot is not used, since a wide empty view differs from a shot by content rather than by colour. The drama's **series look** (`dramas.look`: none, warm, cool, teal_orange, faded, mono) is laid over the joined picture with built-in filters. The sound is levelled with two-pass `loudnorm` to -16 LUFS (true peak -1.5, range 11) in a separate pass that copies the picture. The film's encoder line records the frame, how many clips were matched, the look and the loudness.

## Alternatives Considered

- **A separate `keyframe_prompt` column.** Rejected: nothing wrote `image_prompt`, the studio never showed it, and a second prompt field would leave one unused.
- **Send the keyframe together with the reference images.** Rejected: Ark's guides say the mix may be refused, ModelRunner serves the two through different endpoints, and the keyframe already carries what the references show.
- **End frames (first/last-frame endpoints).** Deferred to the backlog: the schemas are known, but no shot has an end picture to send until chaining exists.
- **Grade towards the scene plate, or towards the previous clip.** Rejected: a plate differs by content, and matching the previous clip lets one bad clip pull the rest.
- **A LUT file per look.** Rejected for now: built-in filters need no assets and stay mild; a LUT picker can replace them later.
- **Single-pass loudnorm.** Rejected: its dynamic mode pumps on short clips; the linear two-pass mode keeps the mix as it is, only louder or quieter.

## Consequences

- Contracts: `Shot.keyframePath`, `ShotCard.latestKeyframeTask`, `UpdateShot.keyframePath`, `RequestShotKeyframe`, `RequestShotVideo.useKeyframe`, `ShotVideoStarted.keyframe`, `ShotVideo.firstFrame`, `VideoProviderCaps.firstFrame`, `Drama.look`, `MergeShots.colorMatch`. Migrations 0009 (`dramas.look`) and 0010 (`shots.keyframe_path`).
- A keyframe costs one image per shot, and a keyframe-started video sends one image instead of several; on a model without first frames the keyframe is only a preview for the creator and a colour target for the merge.
- Ark's `first_frame` role is unverified against the official reference until a paid run; if it is refused, Ark's `firstFrame` cap goes back to false.
- Merges take three FFmpeg passes and a colour read per clip; a long episode renders noticeably slower.

## Amendment (2026-10-17)

**A keyframe says when it is stale.** A keyframe drawn before its shot changed went on as the first frame with nothing to
show it no longer fit. Shots now carry two counters (migration 0014): `keyframe_revision` moves on whenever what a
keyframe is drawn from changes, and `keyframe_drawn_revision` records the revision a keyframe was drawn at (taken when
it was requested, so a change made while it is being drawn leaves it stale) or kept at. The contract's
`Shot.keyframeStale` is derived from the two while the shot has a keyframe; existing keyframes start fresh.

- *What moves the revision:* a changed cast, prop bindings or scene; the image prompt; while the image prompt is empty,
  the framing, angle, location, time, description or video prompt it is composed from; a changed picture (any view or
  the sheet) or styling of a bound character, picture or lighting of the bound scene, or picture of a bound prop,
  including a generated picture landing, a styling or lighting an extraction fills in, and the asset's deletion; a
  changed location of the bound scene, for the shots whose composed keyframe names it (no image prompt and no
  location of their own); and a changed outfit or outfit picture of a bound character in that episode (adr-0023).
  Reordering the same cast or props does not. Changes the keyframe is not drawn from (a character's appearance, which its
  pictures carry; the shot's duration, line or atmosphere) do not.
- *What makes it fresh:* a newly drawn keyframe whose request saw the current revision, an uploaded or removed keyframe,
  and `UpdateShot.keyframeStale: false`, the studio's "Still fits", when the creator judges the change harmless.
- *A signal, not a gate:* a stale keyframe still goes as the first frame. The inspector shows a warning beside it with
  "Still fits"; the keyframe sheet and batch are unchanged.
- *Not covered:* a change of the drama's style or aspect ratio does not mark keyframes stale (backlog).
