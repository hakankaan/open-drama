# Chain inside takes, re-anchor at every cut

Last-frame chaining is a real continuity tool, but a narrow one: a frame carries composition, pose, camera position and the lighting state *as rendered*, and nothing else. It carries no identity model, no scene layout beyond the frame edge, no motion momentum (one frame has no velocity, and generators decelerate at clip ends), no voice, and it carries forward whatever colour and face drift the previous clip already accumulated, which is why every vendor and practitioner source in the notes pairs chaining with re-anchoring to canonical references and caps chain depth at two or three hops. The official BytePlus Seedance 2.0 prompt guide, the single most authoritative document found, says continuation from generated video produces "frame jumps or rollback" at the join and that "multiple continuations will compound the degradation, and mottled color blocks are especially likely to appear in character face regions" ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). For Open Drama the tactic is a new pipeline stage plus a new adapter role, it turns the 4-wide parallel shot generation into a dependency chain for every chained pair, on Seedance it forfeits the reference-image slots (first/last-frame mode and reference mode are mutually exclusive), and it does nothing about the loudest consistency failure the pipeline has today: every shot re-rolls each character's voice. The cheaper, better-evidenced wins come first: split the character reference into a **headshot plus a full-body image** (the official guide explicitly says multi-view sheets like the project's turnaround cause ID drift and "twins"), replace the timed `0-3s:` prompt lines with untimed `Shot 1 / Shot 2` lines on Seedance (the guide says precise timing "is unstable"), re-bind `Name@Image N` on every mention, inject the scene's lighting block verbatim, then pin voices with TTS and lip-sync, then add a CPU-only post pass (fixed conform profile, head/tail trim, colour match to the scene plate, two-pass loudness). Keyframe-first generation for hard shots and chaining for continuous takes come after that, and automated continuity QA last. Seeds, Veo extend chains, per-character LoRAs, self-hosted holistic models, generative bridge shots and per-shot upscaling should be skipped.

## Last-frame chaining carries the frame, not the character

The user's idea is standard practice under the name "chaining", and the sources are unanimous about what it does and does not do. On the positive side, a first-frame image is the strongest single lever for continuity of framing and subject placement: Google describes image-to-video as the mode "for maintaining character consistency across multiple scenes" and its extension mode as generating "based on the final second of the previous clip" to maintain "visual continuity" ([Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-veo-3-1-and-new-creative-controls-in-the-gemini-api)); a practitioner note on image-to-video reproducibility puts it bluntly: "the start frame is the real reproducibility lever, not the seed" ([cliprise seed guide](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/guides/best-practices/seed-values-explained-reproducible-generation-brands)). The Seedance mirror documents the model-native form of the user's exact tactic, `return_last_frame: true`, which "returns a watermark-free PNG for chaining clips" meant to be fed into the next task's first frame ([apiyi Seedance 2](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/seedance2/video-generation.md)). Chaining is therefore not a hack; it is a vendor-anticipated workflow.

What a frame does not carry is the problem. invideo's Seedance FAQ states that start/end-frame methods "carry no context beyond the single frame you upload" and that Seedance's own extend mode "can't accept character or location references" ([invideo Seedance FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-use-seedance-20-reference-to-video-for/)). The mechanism behind drift is that the model "doesn't hold a stable mental model of your character's face, or the room's layout, or the direction the camera was moving ten seconds ago. So when you force length, you get drift" ([FXI Studio](https://www.fxi.studio/tutorials/ai-video-consistent-characters)). Because the next hop sees only the previous output, errors compound: a Medium practitioner reports that re-anchoring each shot to the previous output made the face drift further on every hop and advises "never let the chain feed itself" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@shrutisaagar13/why-your-seedance-2-0-character-keeps-becoming-someone-else-62d7722431f2)); Morphic's guide says each pass degrades image quality, "showing up first as mottled colour on faces" ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-guide)); Veo users describe skin tone shifting, jackets changing colour and backgrounds mutating across extension segments ([veo3ai.io](https://www.veo3ai.io/blog/veo-3-extend-video-beyond-8-seconds-2026)). The oft-quoted rule of thumb that quality "remains excellent through 4–5 extensions" is anecdotal with no test behind it ([aifreeapi](https://www.aifreeapi.com/en/posts/veo-3-extend-video-length)); an operational Seedance continuation skill caps chain depth since the last canonical re-anchor at **2 by default, 3 hard ceiling** ([TypingMind skill](https://www.typingmind.com/skills/emily2040-seedance-continuation)), also a practice rule rather than a measurement. Colour drift is the best-documented component: the Helios long-video paper reports colour shift that "becomes more severe as the generated video grows longer" and keeps the first frame as a global anchor to constrain it ([Helios, arXiv 2603.04379](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04379)), and a ComfyUI node for MiniMax-H3 chains measured "roughly 2.4% per trip" darkening from VAE round-trips, a mechanism hint from a different model rather than a number that transfers ([H3 Chain Normalize](https://comfy.icu/node/H3ChainNormalize)). The research consensus is that drift is exposure bias, training on clean history but conditioning on self-generated frames at test time; models trained on their own rollouts suppress it dramatically, with Rolling Forcing's 30-second drift score falling to **0.01 against 5.59** for a diffusion-forcing baseline ([Rolling Forcing, arXiv 2509.25161](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25161v1)) and Stable Video Infinity holding **98.13% VBench subject consistency at 50 s against 87.03% for naive Wan 2.1 extension** ([SVI, arXiv 2510.09212](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09212v1)). Those are 1.3B–14B research checkpoints, not anything on ModelRunner; the lesson for a commercial-API pipeline is that the model will not correct a chain's errors, so the pipeline must.

There are two seam problems beyond drift. Generators decelerate the camera at clip ends, so naive concatenation reads as a freeze "unless you match momentum and trim the deceleration tail" ([prompt-architects](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/371-first-frame-and-last-frame-control)), and clips carry 0.5–1 s of unstable frames at head and tail that need trimming (practitioner observation, [AI Magicx](https://www.aimagicx.com/blog/long-form-ai-video-character-consistency-guide-2026)). The official Seedance fix for extension joins is editorial: "trim 6 frames from the end of the previous video segment" and "1 frame from the beginning of the following video segment", and better still "end the video at the moment of a transition cut, and start the next video from the new scene after the ending cut" ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). Chinese long-drama practice adds that the previous segment should end on a neutral pose and expression, not mid-action, and that the full appearance description must be repeated on every extension because the model "forgets" ([知乎, 15-second segments](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2007970818878088815)). Note that the official degradation FAQ is specifically about feeding generated *video* back in; the guide gives no numbers for frame-only chaining, so the frame path's drift rate is undocumented by the vendor.

The mitigations that practitioners and papers converge on form a single recipe. First, re-anchor every hop: pass the trimmed last frame as the start frame *and* keep the canonical character and location references in the call, which invideo describes as re-uploading the clipped segment "together with the same character and location references" ([invideo Seedance FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-use-seedance-20-reference-to-video-for/)) and Picassoia as periodically re-introducing the original reference image instead of chaining from the previous scene's output ([Picassoia](https://blog.picassoia.com/how-seedance-2-0-keeps-characters-consistent-across-scenes)). Second, condition on the last second rather than one frame where the model allows it: InfiniteTalk found that sampling reference frames too far apart "weakened control so much that identity and background errors built up" and uses the trailing segment of the previous chunk to "propagate momentum" and avoid the "visible jumps between chunks" of first/last-frame methods ([InfiniteTalk](https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk)); the ComfyUI "Wan Advanced I2V" node takes "tail frames or latents from the previous chunk" for the same reason ([WanAdvancedI2V](https://comfy.icu/node/WanAdvancedI2V)). Third, colour-match each segment to the first shot or the scene plate rather than to its predecessor, which the H3 chain nodes do per RGB channel with an ease-in ([H3 Chain Normalize](https://comfy.icu/node/H3ChainNormalize)). Fourth, hide joins in motion (a turn, a door) and insert a 1–2 s cutaway when a join is harsh ([PixMind](https://www.pixmind.io/posts/ai-video-character-consistency-guide)).

Now the Open Drama arithmetic. Shots are independent calls with nothing fed forward, and up to four run in parallel (`OPEN_DRAMA_VIDEO_CONCURRENCY`); the ModelRunner adapter refuses any endpoint with a required input it cannot fill, which is exactly why every first-frame endpoint (`image`, `image_url`, `start_image_url`) is unreachable today and why LongCat was refused. Chaining therefore needs a "first frame" adapter role, a frame-extraction step (FFmpeg last-frame grab, trim, resize to the first clip's input shape), and an ordering dependency between shots. Making every shot depend on its predecessor would serialise an episode that currently runs four shots at a time, roughly quadrupling wall-clock time for the video stage. But most of Open Drama's shot joins are hard cuts by construction, since a shot is one scene and "a new location or time of day starts a new shot"; chaining across a hard cut propagates drift for no continuity gain, because the next shot starts on a different framing anyway. The vendor framing matches: the Seedance guide reserves "continuous long take (video extension)" for dialogue scenes within a single scene and "segmented stitching" for "plot turns or complex, fast-paced action scenes", and says the two are usually combined ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). So the right unit is not the shot list but a flagged pair: the storyboard breaker marks a shot as "continues previous shot" only for same-scene, continuous-action joins, the job scheduler orders only those pairs and keeps everything else parallel, and chain depth is capped at two or three. On Seedance the chain also costs identity references, because "the three image modes are mutually exclusive, so first/last frame inputs cannot be combined with reference images" and a request mixing them fails ([apiyi Seedance 2](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/seedance2/video-generation.md)), a restriction Runway's generated SDK repeats for Seedance 2 ("the two modes cannot be mixed") ([Runway SDK](https://github.com/runwayml/sdk-node/blob/main/src/resources/image-to-video.ts)) and Wan 3.0 shares ("reference types and first/last-frame types are mutually exclusive") ([Comfy router Wan mirror](https://docs.comfy.org/zh/development/comfy-router/models/wan/wan2-6-r2v/code)). Two gateway-mirrored schemas appear to allow a first frame *and* references in one call, Kling Omni (`image_list` first_frame plus `element_list`) and Wan 2.7 r2v (`media[]` with `first_frame` plus up to five `reference_image`/`reference_video` items) ([OrcaRouter Kling](https://docs.orcarouter.ai/kling-video/overview), [Comfy router Wan mirror](https://docs.comfy.org/zh/development/comfy-router/models/wan/wan2-6-r2v/code)); both are third-party mirrors that must be re-checked on the vendor pages before wiring. Finally, chaining does nothing for audio: even Veo's native extension carries voice only "if it's present in the last 1 second of video" ([Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)), and a manual chain is "a separate render with its own freshly generated sound, so treat each join like a cut" ([atlascloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/veo-3-longer-than-8-seconds)).

Verdict: adopt chaining as a narrow, opt-in, dependency-ordered mechanism for continuous takes, always with references where the provider allows it, with trimmed neutral-pose end frames, a depth cap, and post colour-matching; do not adopt it as the default join between shots. Classification: (c) new stage plus the "first frame" adapter role.

Two structural facts shape the choice of provider. Sora 2 is gone: OpenAI's guide now opens with "the Sora 2 models and Videos API were shut down on September 24, 2026" with "no one-to-one replacement" ([OpenAI video generation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/video-generation)), so any cameo or storyboard pattern from Sora is a design reference only. And Google now positions Gemini Omni Flash as its default video model, keeping Veo 3.1 "for scene extension, last-frame control, or integration with legacy pipelines" ([Gemini API video overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video)); Omni's video-input mode is the natural "continue from the last seconds" candidate but its API shape could not be verified in the notes.

The practical reading of the table is that Open Drama's reference-slot design is already the right abstraction, the same one Kling Elements, Flow Ingredients and Seedance `@Image` references productise, and that the model-native levers worth adding are the ones the contract already half-carries: `referenceAudioUrls`, `referenceVideoUrls`, `seed`, plus three new adapter roles (first frame, last frame, source video, negative prompt). ## Reference assets: one headshot plus one full body, never a sheet

The strongest finding in the notes is an official guideline that contradicts the project's current character asset. Open Drama generates one "turnaround sheet" per character (head-and-shoulders portrait plus full-body front/side/back at the same scale), normalised to fit 768×768 at JPEG quality 68. The BytePlus Seedance 2.0 prompt guide, updated 2026-09-28, says of character references: "using a headshot + full-body photo is sufficient. Using multi-view character images is not recommended. Multi-view assets contain different angles of the same character, and the model may easily identify them as multiple different subjects, which instead worsens the ID drift issue" ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). Its "duplicated characters" FAQ names "three-view character images" as a trigger for twins, and its "inaccurate face reference" FAQ attributes mid-video face swapping to "mixed reference images" (one combined image with face, pose, outfit and detail references) and to "face ratio is too small"; the prescribed fix is a close-up "containing only the character's head... no expression is best; minimize... shoulders, neck, and background", bound in the prompt as "<Subject 1> facial features reference image 1 (headshot), makeup and styling reference image 2 (full-body photo)", with important assets placed first because "the more an asset requires precise reference, the earlier it should be placed" ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). The guide also caps practical assets at "4–5 assets in total: 1–2 character images (facial close-up / full body) + 1 scene image + 1 camera movement video + 1 audio clip", warns that "when the number of reference people exceeds 4, the stability of the model output decreases", and for crowded shots recommends generating a group image of ≤4 people first and then animating it with image-to-video, which is an official endorsement of the keyframe-first workflow for that case.

The small-face problem is compounded by the current normalisation (an inference from the numbers, not a sourced claim): a 9:16 sheet fitted into 768×768 is 432 px wide, so the face panel inside a multi-panel sheet occupies a small fraction of an already small image, whereas the other vendors' floors are 300 px per side (Seedance, Kling), 400 px on the short side (Happy Horse, already recorded as a quirk), and third-party Runway guidance recommends nothing below 640×640 ([Runway inputs page](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/assets/inputs/)). Separating the headshot into its own slot fixes both the "mixed reference" and the "face ratio" failure at once. Practitioners corroborate without official backing: PixMind reports that once the face falls below roughly 20% of frame the model fills in a generic face (anecdotal) ([PixMind](https://www.pixmind.io/posts/ai-video-character-consistency-guide)), Kling's Element Library recommends "a front-facing image for higher consistency" with 2–4 separate images per element and descriptions that state "features the model should ignore" ([Kling Element Library](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-element-library-3-user-guide)), PixVerse says "subject images should isolate the main subject clearly" ([PixVerse Fusion how-to](https://docs.platform.pixverse.ai/how-to-use-fusionreference-to-video-1339253m0)), and Google Flow's help says a consistent look across all ingredient images helps the model blend them ([Google Flow Help](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353334?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)). The one dissent is the practitioner habit of multi-angle turnarounds (front, three-quarter, profile, back, face detail) as the *starting* artefact ([PixMind](https://www.pixmind.io/posts/ai-video-character-consistency-guide), [AI Magicx](https://www.aimagicx.com/blog/long-form-ai-video-character-consistency-guide-2026)); that is compatible with the official advice if the sheet is used to *derive* the clean headshot and full-body stills and is never itself passed to the video model. The weak quantitative support for locked references over prompt-only description is PixMind's internal 30-shot test, **24/30 stable faces with a locked reference plus identity block against 9/30 prompt-only** (self-reported) ([PixMind](https://www.pixmind.io/posts/ai-video-character-consistency-guide)), and a Chinese creator's 10-episode average of 67% per-shot consistency with a single reference (promotional, unverifiable) ([Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1004426221_122690230)).

This maps cleanly onto Open Drama's series model. The headshot becomes the series-constant identity asset, generated once and reused by the deduplicating extraction across episodes; the full-body image becomes the per-episode styling asset, which is what the project already intends with "as worn in this episode" wardrobe. Practitioners state the rule as "a change of state is a new character": wet hair, a stained shirt or a second outfit get their own reference and descriptor ([invideo reference-images FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-use-reference-images-in-ai-video-generation/)). Scene assets follow the same logic. The one-plate-per-place-at-time-of-day design matches "lock location plates... attach them to every generation so the environment holds shot to shot" ([invideo Seedance FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-use-seedance-20-reference-to-video-for/)); the practitioner upgrade is several angles per location because "the moment the camera moves it collapses" (search summary, [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2012466186349209444)), and the scene's `lighting` field should become a verbatim block in every shot prompt, since switching wording mid-scene ("warm lamp light" in shot 1, "amber glow" in shot 4) "is what causes drift" and named sources with hex palette anchors reproduce where adjectives do not ([invideo lighting FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-maintain-consistent-lighting-across-multiple/)). Research supports background references as a conditioning input: MultiShotMaster takes "a customized scene driven by a background image" and SkyReels-A2 and MAGREF accept background elements ([MultiShotMaster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03041), [SkyReels-A2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02436), [MAGREF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23742)), which is the published form of what the scene slot already does.

Classification: headshot plus full-body is (b), a `character-prompt` skill change plus a second image on the character record (the reference-slot list already accepts multiple slots per shot, so no adapter work); scene plate sets and the lighting block are (b) skill and prompt-assembly changes; the multi-view sheet should be retired from the video request entirely. Remaining unknowns: no vendor states a recommended reference resolution, and Kling, Wan, MiniMax, Vidu and PixVerse publish no face-versus-body guidance at all.

## Prompt discipline and keyframe-first planning do the heavy lifting

Open Drama's video prompt is a header with `@[Name]` mentions followed by "one line per 3-second segment" starting `0-3s:`, `3-6s:` (`workspace/skills/prompt-generator/video-prompt/SKILL.md`). The official Seedance guide prescribes the opposite for sub-shots: "use identifiers such as Shot 1, Shot 2, and Shot 3 to organize content in the order events occur", "do not impose strict limits on the duration of each segment", because "the model's support for precise timing (such as 0–3 seconds) is unstable, and forcibly limiting duration may lead to abnormal generation results" ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)). The guide's worked example is itself a "Dormitory emotional short drama (dialogue-focused)", with assets bound as `@Image 1` (half-body lead), `@Image 2` (dormitory scene), `@Video 1` (camera reference), `@Audio 1` (ambience), shot lines ("Shot 1: ... girl @Image 1 walks ..."), dialogue in braces, and a closing constraint "The character's face remains stable without deformation; movements are natural and smooth, with no stutter or flicker." Other rules from the same document: bind the subject on every appearance ("each time a subject is involved, it must be explicitly referred to", form `Zhang San@Image 1`), one camera movement per shot ("specify only 1 type of camera movement in a single shot" or instability increases), prefer "slow, gentle, continuous small movements", append anti-subtitle, anti-logo and anti-watermark sentences (subtitles are likelier in portrait than landscape), keep the dialogue language consistent and tag non-Chinese/English lines, add explicit style words when references are realistic to avoid live-action drift, and "do not directly use the complete script as the prompt". The Sora cookbook, historical but the only other official prompting document, reaches the same recipe: "keep descriptions consistent across shots, reuse phrasing for continuity", one camera move and one subject action per shot, three to five named colours to hold the palette ([Sora 2 prompting guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)); LTX's guide adds "reuse the same visual identifiers for recurring people or objects" and "one coherent light logic per shot" ([LTX prompting guide](https://docs.ltx.io/api-documentation/implementation-guides/prompting-guide.md)). Since mention tokens are already rendered per provider, the sub-shot line format can be rendered per provider too (untimed `Shot N` lines for Seedance, timed lines kept where a provider tolerates them), and the style preset prefix already covers the style-words rule. Two provider knobs belong with this: turn Wan's `prompt_extend` / `enable_prompt_expansion` off so the preset text is not rewritten between shots ([Bailian Wan 2.7](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/model-studio/image-to-video-general-api-reference)), and pin and record the exact model version per drama, since practitioners include "exact version numbers in your lock command" ([invideo character consistency blog](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-character-consistency-video/)). All of this is (b): skill and adapter changes with no new stage.

Keyframe-first is the dominant practitioner pipeline of 2026 and the one with the only comparative numbers. A workflow guide calls prompting straight into a video generator "a gamble" and recommends generating key frames before animating anything ([FutureFlow AI](https://www.futureflowai.in/post/how-to-make-a-cinematic-ai-short-film-in-2026-the-complete-workflow)); invideo's "anchor frame" method generates one locked keyframe at the target location and feeds both the sheet and the frame into Seedance reference-to-video ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-character-consistency-video/)); a Veo guide says to upload the actual still "since Veo respects the reference better than rambling descriptions" ([PC Tech Magazine](https://pctechmag.com/2026/04/nano-banana-2-veo-3-1-the-ultimate-ai-drama-workflow/)); Chinese drama manuals use single-keyframe image-to-video for easy shots and first/last-frame pairs only for hard ones, with a fail-and-regenerate log (search summary, [Zhihu Seedance manual](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2033803179847104022)). The academic tables are the useful evidence. In Long Context Tuning's scene-level comparison, the keyframe-first baseline **StoryDiffusion + Kling scored the highest consistency average, 96.57, above LCT's own 95.65**, with LCT winning only on text alignment and the human ranking ([LCT, arXiv 2503.10589](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10589v1)); in HoloCine's table, naive per-shot Wan 2.2 scores **0.6772 inter-shot consistency, keyframe-first baselines lift it to 0.7096–0.7364, and the holistic model reaches 0.7509**, with holistic generation's real edge in transition control (0.98 against 0.48) rather than identity ([HoloCine, arXiv 2510.20822](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20822v1)). Planned keyframes recover most of the gap to joint generation, and the rest needs models Open Drama cannot run (HoloCine trained on 128 H800s). What no source provides is a controlled test of keyframe-then-I2V against direct multi-reference reference-to-video; Flick's preference for start/end frames is qualitative ([Flick](https://flick.art/blog/img2img-consistent-character)).

For Open Drama, keyframe-first is a small new stage because the pieces exist. `google/nano-banana-2.1/edit` combines up to 14 reference images into one new image "keeping up to 4 characters and 10 objects consistent" at about $0.034 per image, and the image adapter already pairs `text-to-image` endpoints with `edit` siblings and sends `referenceImages`, so composing a per-shot keyframe from the headshot, the full-body image and the scene plate needs a pipeline step and a shot field, not an adapter role ([ModelRunner docs](https://modelrunner.ai/docs)). The I2V step then needs the same "first frame" adapter role as chaining. The payoffs stack: the keyframe bakes identity into the frame, which sidesteps the Seedance reference-versus-first-frame exclusivity; it gives Veo a path to shots longer than reference mode allows (I2V still caps at 8 s, but Happy Horse I2V runs 3–15 s and Wan 2.7 2–15 s); it is the natural colour-match reference for post; and it makes the storyboard reviewable as a contact sheet before any video credit is spent, which several guides describe as the point of the exercise. First/last-frame pairs should stay reserved for shots whose end composition matters (a cut into a known frame), because of the documented hold/fade/morph failure when frames disagree. Classification: (c), but cheap.

Generation order is the last piece. Keep shot-level generation parallel and reference-anchored; chain only flagged continuous-take pairs; never chain from drifted output alone. A middle option practitioners use, generating a scene's establishing shot first and promoting an approved frame to the scene plate for the remaining shots (Runway's "scene expansion", feeding "strong outputs back in as new references") ([Heather Cooper](https://heatherbcooper.substack.com/p/new-tools-for-ultimate-character), [imagine.art](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/prompt-guide-runway-gen-4-references)), introduces a two-phase order per scene at the cost of one serial hop; it is worth having as an option for the scene plate, not for character identity, given the Medium report that re-anchoring faces to previous outputs drifts them further.

## Voice is the one layer chaining cannot fix

Open Drama has no TTS, no voice id and no lip-sync; each model generates its own dialogue per shot, so the same character's voice is re-rolled every shot and every episode. No video API in the notes offers a voice id to pin a speaker across clips (Kling 3.0 Omni's voice binding is the closest, third-party documented, with the field that attaches a `voice_id` to a video request unverified). The only reliable fix is to stop letting the video model invent the voice: synthesise each line with a TTS voice id stored on the character record, then either drive the picture from that audio or generate silent and lip-sync afterwards. The catalog already has the pieces. `resemble-ai/chatterbox/text-to-speech` clones from a short recording with emotion control at ~$0.004–0.005/s, `elevenlabs/tts/multilingual-v2` at ~$0.015/s and `minimax/speech-02-hd` at ~$0.006/s all expose stable voices ([ModelRunner docs](https://modelrunner.ai/docs)); vendor-side, any ElevenLabs library voice can be referenced by ID "including cloned voices, and voices you've designed" ([ElevenLabs API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)), and MiniMax charges $3 per designed voice and $1.5 per rapid clone, billed on first use ([MiniMax pricing](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/pricing-paygo)). Voice design from a text description suits fictional characters because no real-person sample is needed. Chinese drama dubbing practice converges on the same rule: give each main character "a fixed custom or realistic AI voice and keep it across episodes unless the character ages", generate lines in storyboard-sized chunks with emotion labels, and treat pauses as more important than emotion settings ([塔猴 dubbing guide](https://www.tahou.com/article/212318660130519045)). A 12-second line is roughly 150 characters, so per-shot TTS cost is around a cent on any vendor.

Attaching the voice to the picture has three routes, in order of cleanliness. Audio-driven generation needs no post step: `wan-video/wan/v2.7/image-to-video/audio-driven` turns a still plus your audio into a 2–15 s lip-synced clip ($2.25/run avg), Bailian's Wan 2.7 accepts `driving_audio` (2–30 s) and `first_frame + last_frame + driving_audio` combinations ([Bailian Wan 2.7](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/model-studio/image-to-video-general-api-reference)), and Wan 2.5/2.6 take an `audio_url` next to `img_url` ([Alibaba Wan API reference](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/legacy-image-to-video-api-reference/)). Post lip-sync works on any provider's output: `bytedance/latentsync` is the catalog's cheapest at $0.014/s (about $0.17 for a 12 s shot), `pixverse/lipsync` $0.24/s, `sync/lipsync/v2` $0.32/s ([ModelRunner docs](https://modelrunner.ai/docs)); Sync's own docs warn that "multiple speakers in frame" and "extreme profile view faces" degrade results and that lip-sync "will not work" on still-frame portions, with sync-3 the tier that handles profiles ([sync.so lipsync models](https://sync.so/docs/models/lipsync)); Kling's lip-sync accepts only 2–10 s inputs at ~12 minutes of fixed processing ([fal Kling lipsync](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/kling-video/lipsync/audio-to-video)), so 15 s shots need chunking or routing. Style-only reference is the fallback: Seedance `reference_audio` lets the model "lock in ... voice-over timbre" per the official guide, but the same guide's FAQ says the generated voice "can differ from reference audio" and recommends describing the voice in words as well ([BytePlus prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-2-0-prompt-guide)), and third parties call it "a style guide, not voice cloning" ([ClipDance](https://clipdance.ai/blog/seedance-2-consistent-voice)). Veo always generates audio with no documented voice input or off switch (third-party claim, unverified against Google docs) ([invideo Kling 3 vs Veo 3.1](https://invideo.io/blog/kling-3-vs-veo-3-1/)), so Veo and MiniMax H3 shots take the strip-and-replace path. The muting side is already in hand: `generateAudio: false` exists in the contract, and the project has even seen Seedance pass with it after rejecting its own soundtrack.

Two design consequences follow. Because the line now exists before the video, its measured duration can set the shot's requested duration so mouth motion and line length agree, the "audio length is the timing master" principle of an open-source drama pipeline ([AI-Drama-Pipeline](https://github.com/alexsa2517/AI-Drama-Pipeline)). And multi-speaker shots are the weak point of every lip-sync product, so the storyboard breaker should prefer single-speaker shots and over-the-shoulder cuts for dialogue, a skill change rather than code. A 5–15 s clean sample rendered from the pinned TTS voice itself doubles as the `referenceAudioUrls` input for Seedance, MiniMax H3 and Kling Omni, so no human recording is needed.

Music and ambience are cheaper still and belong in the same stage: one episode-level music bed (`stability-ai/stable-audio-2.5/text-to-audio` at $0.20 per generation up to ~190 s on ModelRunner; Lyria 2 at $0.06 per 30 s on Vertex ([Vertex pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing))), ducked under dialogue with `sidechaincompress`, joined with `acrossfade`, finished with two-pass EBU R128 `loudnorm` in linear mode (single-pass runs dynamic mode and alters dynamics) ([dev.to loudnorm guide](https://dev.to/javidjamae/ffmpeg-loudnorm-filter-ebu-r128-loudness-normalization-guide-15d4)) followed by `aresample=48000` because loudnorm resamples internally to 192 kHz ([FFmpeg patchwork](https://patchwork.ffmpeg.org/patch/10029/)). Genflick measured loudness jumps at every cut across 394 clips and 26 films, though only the summary is public ([Genflick benchmarks](https://genflick.com/benchmarks)). Classification: the voice id, TTS step and mux are (c); `referenceAudioUrls` and `generateAudio` routing are (b); the pipeline's `generateAudio` flag should become a per-model capability (native, audio-input, always-on) so the pipeline knows whether to pass the track in, request silence, or strip afterwards.

## Post-production and QA close the residual gap

Compositing today is an FFmpeg `concat` after scaling and padding every clip into the first clip's frame, with no colour work, transitions, loudness or frame-rate harmonisation (`apps/api/src/modules/compositing/render.ts`). Practitioners run a fixed chain on every clip for a reason: AI clips shift in colour "because each generation is rendered independently, so temperature, contrast and saturation can change between batches even with the same prompt" ([invideo colour-grade FAQ](https://invideo.co/faq/how-do-you-color-grade-ai-generated-video-clips-to-look/)), and Genflick measured colour drift at every cut across 124 clips in 12 scenes, concluding "one correction per clip is still enough" (summary only) ([Genflick benchmarks](https://genflick.com/benchmarks)). The CPU-only version of that chain is: trim the unstable head and tail (0.5–1 s per practitioners, or the official 6-frame/1-frame trim at extension joins), conform every clip to a fixed series profile (for example 1080×1920, 24 fps, yuv420p) rather than to the first clip's frame so episode N+1 matches episode N and `xfade` becomes possible, colour-match each clip to the scene plate or the shot's keyframe rather than to the previous clip (matching to the predecessor only chains drift), and apply one series look LUT and a uniform grain layer on top. The `color-matcher` package implements Reinhard, Monge-Kantorovich and MVGD transfer plus histogram matching on sampled frames, with the compound `hm-mkl-hm` claimed to beat the single methods, GPL-3.0, no direct video input ([hahnec/color-matcher](https://github.com/hahnec/color-matcher)); statistics-only matching (`eq` and `colorbalance` toward the reference's Y/U/V means) is the safer first step because full histogram transfer can shift skin tones when the reference composition differs from the shot. Cut smoothing should stay the exception: FFmpeg `xfade` needs identical resolution, fps and pixel format and its offsets accumulate when chaining more than two clips ([FFmpeg wiki Xfade](https://trac.ffmpeg.org/wiki/Xfade)); RIFE and FILM "only blend between frames that already exist" and ghost when pose or framing differ ([ComfyUI wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/models/frame-interpolation/frame-interpolation)); a generative first/last-frame bridge costs a full generation and adds a shot the script did not ask for, so it fits scene transitions only. No practitioner source described frame interpolation at cuts; motion-hidden cuts and cutaways were the tools named. In drama, most cuts should remain hard cuts.

Uniform upscaling is the one post tactic the numbers argue against as a per-shot step. `topazlabs/upscale/video` on ModelRunner is ~$0.135/s, so a 12 s shot costs about $1.60, comparable to or above the generation itself; SeedVR2 is $0.001 per output megapixel on fal (about $0.60 for a 12 s 1080p shot) and its authors warn it over-sharpens lightly degraded inputs "such as 720p AIGC videos" ([ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR), [fal SeedVR2](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/seedvr/upscale/video)). No source quantified the benefit of uniform upscaling or grain; the ordering "upscale, then blur and grain, then grade" rests on vendor advice ([invideo colour-grade FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-color-grade-ai-generated-video-clips-to-look/)). If used at all, run it once on the assembled episode or only on clips below the target resolution. All of this is (c), a small FFmpeg stage with one optional Python helper.

Automated continuity QA is feasible, cheap, and unvalidated. No commercial studio product exposes a detector; LTX Studio's and Dreamina's consistency features are preventive, and reviewers run manual checks ([WaveSpeed LTX review](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-workflows/ltx-studio-review/)). The research metrics are VBench subject consistency (DINO cosine similarity of each frame to the first and previous frame, chosen because DINO "is not trained to disregard the differences within subjects of the same class") and background consistency (CLIP), both of which must be read with a dynamic-degree score because static videos score high ([VBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17982)); identity in the reference-to-video papers is ArcFace face similarity, and a benchmark tracks per-subject ArcFace embeddings across 32 sampled frames with a rolling average ([MBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00793)). The 2026 multi-shot benchmarks add VLM-judge rubrics: MSVBench scores "character identity, scene layout, and object scale across shot transitions on a 1–5 scale, with explicit anchors for each level" ([MSVBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23969)), MSAVBench scores illumination and colour consistency with rubric-based VLM judges over sampled frames ([MSAVBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20183)), and judge-human agreement is moderate (LogiShot Spearman 0.59–0.68 ([LogiShot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08820)), PersonaShot up to 0.75 with distilled judges, with the warning that zero-shot VLMs "conflate visual quality with structural correctness" ([PersonaShot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16717))). A diagnostic paper finds the strongest open 7–8B video-LLMs reach only 37–38% on character-tracking tasks ([Do Video-LLMs Actually Watch?](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11078)), so the LLM should judge wardrobe, props, extras and lighting jumps against the character sheet and scene plate, and the face embedding should judge identity; do not ask the LLM "is this the same person". Gemini prices the frames at about 300 tokens per second of video at 1 fps, fractions of a cent per shot ([Gemini video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). Two cautions: no validated ArcFace threshold exists for AI-generated faces (0.35 appears only as a training-data filter, and profile frames produce false alarms unless compared like-for-like ([arXiv 2508.09476](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09476))), and InsightFace's model packs are licensed for non-commercial research only ([deepinsight/insightface](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface)), which matters for a shipped product. The sensible shape is a per-shot QA job that emits scores to a review queue with a "regenerate with the same seed and references, up to three attempts" action, mirroring the five-binary-check, three-attempt protocol reviewers use ([WaveSpeed LTX review](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-workflows/ltx-studio-review/)), rather than an automatic gate; the same frame set yields the first/last-frame histogram distances that flag `xfade` candidates and the face detection that gates lip-sync. Classification: (c), and the EntityBench-style "entity schedule" the papers use as expected state is essentially Open Drama's shot list, reusable as the QA input ([EntityBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15199)).

## What to build first, and what to skip

The ordering below weighs evidence strength (official vendor guidance and primary-source tables above practitioner consensus above self-reported numbers), cost in code and credits, and the drama-specific tradeoffs stated throughout. Between-episode consistency rides on the same list: the series-constant headshot, pinned model version, series conform profile, series look LUT, series music theme and per-character voice id are the artefacts that make episode N+1 look and sound like episode N.

| Priority | Tactic | Class | Evidence | Cost and tradeoff | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headshot + full-body per character, face-first asset order, retire the turnaround sheet from video requests; headshot series-constant, full-body per episode | (b) skill + second character image | Official (BytePlus); practitioner corroboration; PixMind 24/30 vs 9/30 self-reported | One extra image per character; no adapter work | Adopt now |
| 2 | Seedance prompt discipline: untimed `Shot N` lines, `Name@Image N` on every mention, constraint sentences, braces for dialogue, language tag, one camera move per sub-shot; lighting block from the scene asset injected verbatim; ≤4 people and 4–5 assets per call | (b) skill + per-provider renderer | Official (BytePlus); Sora/LTX official guides agree | Free | Adopt now |
| 3 | Persist `seed` and model version per shot; negative-prompt adapter role (Kling/Wan/Veo); Wan prompt expansion off | (b) adapter | Vendor docs | Free; improves retakes, not cross-shot identity | Adopt now, expect no continuity gain from seeds |
| 4 | Post v1: fixed series conform profile, head/tail trim (6/1 frames at chained joins), colour match to scene plate or keyframe, series LUT, two-pass `loudnorm` + `aresample` | (c) small FFmpeg stage | Practitioner consensus; Genflick measurements (summary); FFmpeg docs | CPU only; GPL helper as subprocess | Adopt next |
| 5 | Voice pinning: `voiceId` on the character, TTS per line, silent generation or strip, LatentSync/Wan audio-driven; line length sets shot duration; single-speaker dialogue shots; `referenceAudioUrls` as interim style anchor | (c) stage; (b) for the audio fields | Vendor docs; only fix for per-shot voice re-roll | ~$0.01 TTS + ~$0.17 LatentSync per 12 s shot; lip-sync clip caps need chunking | Adopt after 1–4; largest perceptible between-episode gain |
| 6 | Keyframe-first for hard shots: nano-banana keyframe from headshot + body + plate, first-frame adapter role, I2V; contact-sheet review before spend; FLF only where the end frame matters | (c) stage + adapter role | LCT/HoloCine tables; official BytePlus for >4 people; practitioner consensus; no controlled test vs direct R2V | $0.034 per keyframe + I2V; sidesteps Seedance exclusivity and Veo's 8 s reference cap | Adopt as opt-in |

| 8 | Automated QA: ArcFace vs headshot, DINO/CLIP intra-shot, anchored-rubric VLM pass, colour stats; review queue with 3-attempt regenerate | (c) stage | Research metrics; moderate judge-human agreement; no validated thresholds; licence issue | Fractions of a cent per shot | Adopt last, as a signal not a gate |

Skip, with reasons. Seeds as a continuity lever: every vendor says "similar, not identical" and none claims cross-shot value. Veo extend chains: 720p, Veo-made input only, 8 s forced, one growing file, voice only from the last second, unpriced on the gateway. First/last-frame for every shot: the documented hold/fade/morph failure when frames disagree, and Veo's fixed 8 s. The multi-view turnaround sheet as a video reference: officially counter-productive. Per-character LoRAs: 12–24 GB+ VRAM and hours per character on musubi-tuner ([musubi-tuner docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner)), with no controlled study showing they beat reference conditioning across shots; the only ~0.9 consistency figure is a promotional self-report. Self-hosted holistic or autoregressive models (HoloCine, LCT, SVI, LongLive): the strongest route in principle but 14B Wan-class weights or 1.3B research quality, and nothing in the current provider stack. 3D world caches (GEN3C, Voyager, Matrix-3D): the only route to true same-geometry locations, far too heavy for the pipeline's shape. Generative bridge shots and RIFE at cuts: full-generation cost or ghosting for a join that should usually stay hard. Per-shot upscaling: costs as much as generation and over-sharpens clean 720p output. Anything built on Sora 2: shut down.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. Open Drama's consistency problem is not that shots are generated independently; the per-shot, reference-anchored design is what the vendors recommend and what the keyframe-first academic baselines score highest with. The problem is that the references and prompts the pipeline sends are, in two specific ways, the ones the official Seedance guide warns against (a multi-view sheet and timed segment lines), and that the audio layer has no anchor at all. Fixing those is a skill edit and a data-model tweak, not a new stage, and it should land before any chaining work because chaining amplifies whatever identity signal it is given. Last-frame chaining then finds its proper place as a continuity tool for continuous takes inside a scene, scheduled as flagged pairs rather than a serial episode, never fed by its own drifted output, and always followed by a post pass that matches colour to a canonical plate.

The deeper implication is that the keyframe, not the frame grab, is the unit that unlocks the rest: a per-shot still composed from the headshot, body and plate is at once the identity anchor that survives Seedance's mode exclusivity, the start frame for providers whose reference mode is too short or too rigid, the colour-match reference for post, the contact sheet for review before credits are spent, and the expected state a QA job compares against. Building the first-frame adapter role once serves chaining, keyframe-first and audio-driven generation together, which is why it is worth doing even though the chaining it enables should be used sparingly.
